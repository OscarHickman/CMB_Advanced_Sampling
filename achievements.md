## Validated foundations

- **Phase 0 — unlensed Gibbs baseline on real Planck data (lmax=300, float64): converged and trusted.** C_ℓ R-hat median 1.026, 0% of multipoles above 1.1; ~48% sampling efficiency. This is the reference everything downstream is checked against.
- **The float32 false-convergence trap was caught and became a standing precision rule**: C_ℓ R-hat can look converged while the alm block's R-hat blows up by orders of magnitude from gradient noise. fp64 end-to-end unless a mixed scheme is explicitly validated.
- **Phase 1 — differentiable lensing operator**: custom TF implementation, gradients w.r.t. both alm and φ validated against finite differences and an independent FD-free gold-standard Jacobian (exact linear deflection-field via unit-impulse alm × analytic weight gradients).
- **Phase 1.5 — dense SHT matrix eliminated (the hard gate)**: matrix-free ducc0 SHT behind `tf.custom_gradient`, forward+backward 0.018s at lmax=300 (~500× under the gate; the dense path was 9.38s and didn't fit in GPU memory). CG symmetry/linearity/PD checks pass matrix-free; the lensing op is graph-traceable.
- **HMC + matrix-free-SHT pivot for the alm sampler — validated 2026-07-13.** Replaces the non-viable messenger-field route (below) with plain HMC at ~0.34-0.6s/sweep (~600-1000× cheaper than messenger), unlocked by a one-line JIT-compile fix (`model.py::psi_tf` disables JIT when `use_matrixfree_sht=True`, since XLA cannot compile through the `tf.py_function` ducc0 escape hatch, commit `e99fdb2`). Multi-chain R-hat gate passed: C_ℓ R-hat max=1.030, median=1.005, 0% above 1.1 — better than the Phase 0 dense-SHT reference's own 4-chain result (max=1.014, median=1.001). alm-block R-hat is high in both the pivot and the reference (median 1.45 vs 1.60) — not a regression, expected gradient-noise behaviour at n_alm≈90k. Net ESS/wall-clock throughput ~31× better than the dense-SHT reference despite ~4× worse per-sample efficiency (cheaper sweeps more than compensate). **Burn-in investigation and SHT audit along the way (apples-to-oranges false alarm, not a bug):** several burn-in lengths were tried before landing on the above — `n_burnin=500` showed severe unresolved drift at l≥200; `n_burnin=3000` fixed l≤150 but l=200-299 still drifted; `n_burnin=6000` (no MAP init) gave median ESS 80.6% but C_ℓ came out systematically 3-6x low vs the Phase 0 reference. ## Coverage-test prerequisite work (Priority 1) — findings 2026-07-30

- **SETTLED (a scope constraint on the headline deliverable, not a bug): strict simulation-based calibration is impossible for the spectrum blocks, because their implied priors are improper.** Block 1's exact conditional is `C_ℓ|alm ~ InvGamma(ℓ-0.5, S_ℓ/2)`, whose density goes as `C^{-ℓ-0.5} exp(-S_ℓ/2C)`. The C_ℓ likelihood goes as `C^{-(2ℓ+1)/2} exp(-S_ℓ/2C)` — the same thing. So the conditional *is* the likelihood, i.e. the implied prior on C_ℓ is flat and improper; Block 4 has identical structure for C_L^φφ. Strict SBC requires drawing `θ_true ~ p(θ)`, which an improper prior does not permit, so **there is no `C_ℓ_true ~ p(C_ℓ)` to rank**. Consequence for `ROADMAP.md`'s Priority-1 wording ("rank statistic of the true (C_ℓ, C_L^φφ, φ)"): only the **fields** (alm, φ) can carry a strict SBC rank — they are genuine draws from exactly-known Gaussians at fixed fiducial spectra. The spectra get an **interval-coverage** check against *realized* power (`compute_sl_phi_np`, never the CAMB ensemble mean — see the cosmic-variance bug above), which is a weaker claim and must be labelled as coverage rather than calibration in any figure caption. A residual honest caveat on the field ranks: Blocks 1/4 resample the spectra during the chain, so the target marginalises over spectra while the truth was generated at fixed fiducial — second-order (the spectrum posterior concentrates near the generating fiducial) but not exactly zero, so mild rank non-uniformity is not by itself proof of a sampler bug. Documented in `scripts/aggregate_coverage_ranks.py`'s header, which is where the statistic is computed.
- **OPEN (fix submitted, not yet validated): a cold alm start freezes the φ block outright — Block 3/Block 4 runaway, job 11663105, lmax=128.** The first coverage-test equilibration pilot (`scripts/pilot_coverage_equilibration.py`, cold independent prior-draw alm start, `n_burnin=100`, no MAP pre-solve, Block 4 on) produced a completely dead φ block and was cancelled after ~1.5h once the mechanism was clear. Evidence: **φ accept rate 0.004** overall and **0.000 over the last 50 sweeps** (~2 accepted moves in 450); binned φ power at L=2-6 **bit-identical** across sweeps 0/50/150/300 (2.6777e-07); the frozen φ state carrying realized `C_{L=2} ≈ 2.69e-06` against a fiducial `1.12e-08` (**~240x**, which a 5-mode Gaussian draw cannot plausibly produce); adapted alm step ending at 0.457 (vs a healthy 0.128 at lmax=300) with alm accept 0.058, and `phi_step` collapsed to 1.9e-05 (~75x below its start). **Block 4 is exonerated** — its draws tracked the realized power of the φ state it was handed to within the normal inverse-Gamma spread (per-L ratios 1-5), i.e. it faithfully fits a bad φ rather than generating one. Mechanism: a badly wrong alm leaves a large residual, the φ block inflates its low-L amplitude to absorb it, Block 4 follows that inflated φ, the φ mass matrix is rebuilt every sweep from the inflated spectrum, and φ locks at ~0 accept — a self-reinforcing trap with no route back. **This did not show up in Block 4's own 2026-07-29 validation because that was warm-started at truth, where the loop has no room to run away** — a reminder that truth-warm-started validation cannot certify cold-start behaviour. Fix in flight: the pilot now takes the **data-driven MAP** alm start (`find_map_estimate`, `samplers.py:54` — the pattern `scripts/run_sampler.py` already uses, and which the 2026-07-13 burn-in false alarm above had *already* identified as this codebase's answer to burn-in-to-mode time) with burn-in raised 100→400, on fresh `*_v2_*` checkpoint paths so the frozen state can never be resumed into. Note the MAP start is **not** a truth leak: `find_map_estimate` descends on the data alone and never sees `alm_true`/`phi_true`, which is what makes it legitimate for a calibration test where a truth start is not. A **φ accept-rate floor (0.15)** was added as a first-class gate in the pilot's verdict, ahead of the trace statistics, because a frozen chain makes every autocorrelation/drift number a measurement of the wrong thing.

- **FIXED, 2026-07-31: the φ-block memory leak was eager-mode `tf.py_function` registration, not a ducc0/geometry resource — root-caused by isolated reproduction, fixed by tracing the HMC step, and verified.** Following up on the RSS probe below: a minimal reproduction (`scripts/debug_phi_memory_leak.py`) calling `psi_lensed`'s value+gradient in a bare eager loop (no HMC/Gibbs machinery) reproduced the leak on its own (~1 MB/call at lmax=32/nside=32, 1200 calls). Wrapping that same computation in `@tf.function` **traced successfully** (contradicting the 2026-07-01 comment claiming it "cannot be traced" — a since-superseded assumption from before the matrix-free SHT path's `tf.py_function`-wrapped escape hatches existed) and **eliminated the leak entirely** (flat RSS, ~35% faster too — eager `tf.py_function` calls register a callback in TF's process-global eager py-function registry every invocation and are never freed, a documented TF footgun; a traced graph registers once and is reused). A further test with the *actual* `tfp.mcmc.HamiltonianMonteCarlo.one_step`, still calling `bootstrap_results` eagerly every sweep (matching production's per-sweep re-bootstrap, needed because Gibbs conditioning changes between blocks), still leaked (~1.35 MB/sweep at phi_n_lfs=20) — isolating the residual leak to that one eager call. Combining `bootstrap_results` + `one_step` into a single `@tf.function`, traced once and called every sweep, eliminated that too (flat RSS across 300 sweeps). **The complication, and why this wasn't a trivial `@tf.function` slap-on**: `log_prob_phi_block`'s `cl_phiphi_full` argument was a plain numpy array, rebound (not `.assign()`-ed) fresh every sweep whenever Block 4 (`sample_cl_phiphi=True`) is active — closed over by `phi_log_prob_whitened` and converted via `tf.constant(...)` inside `log_prob_phi_block`. Naively wrapping the phi step in `@tf.function` without addressing this would have **silently frozen the target's φ-prior spectrum at whatever `cl_phiphi_full` held at first-trace time**, ignoring every subsequent Block 4 update — a serious, silent correctness bug, not just a missed optimization. Fixed by (1) changing `log_prob_phi_block`'s internal conversion from `tf.constant` to `tf.cast(tf.convert_to_tensor(...), tf.float64)` (identical behaviour for numpy-array callers, but reads a `tf.Variable` live at call time instead of baking in its trace-time value), and (2) introducing `cl_phiphi_full_var`, a `tf.Variable` twin of `cl_phiphi_full`, `.assign()`-ed alongside the existing numpy rebind in Step 3a, and passed to `log_prob_phi_block` in place of the raw array. Applied uniformly: both the `hmc`+phi and `cg`+phi branches now use combined `hmc_bootstrap_and_step`/`phi_hmc_bootstrap_and_step` `@tf.function`s (the alm block was fixed too — its `bootstrap_results` ran eagerly regardless of `sample_phi`, a smaller version of the same bug). **Verified**: full test suite 86 passed/1 skipped (no regressions, including `test_gibbs_chain_sample_cl_phiphi_recovers_known_spectrum`, which would very plausibly have failed if the frozen-spectrum bug above had been introduced instead of avoided); a dedicated real-`run_gibbs_chain` regression at lmax=24-32 with `sample_cl_phiphi=True` confirmed `cl_phiphi_samples` continues to vary sweep-to-sweep (95.5% distinct phi-power trace values, no frozen/stuck signature) and all outputs stay finite. **RSS-flatness confirmed at the actual failing configuration** (lmax=32/nside=32, phi_n_lfs=80, Block 4 on, 200 sweeps, ONE uninterrupted `run_gibbs_chain` call with background-thread RSS sampling every 1s — the same methodology as the original pre-fix probe): RSS shows a single one-time ~67 MB step around t=92-117s (a normal TF allocator arena-growth event, not a sustained trend — happens once, at a consistent wall-clock point, unrelated to sweep count) and is then **completely flat** for the remaining ~150s/~110 sweeps (834.0→835.9 MB, ≈17 KB/sweep — allocator noise, not a leak). Against the pre-fix ~362 MB/sweep, this is roughly a **20,000× reduction**, i.e. the leak is gone in the regime that matters. (An earlier, flawed verification attempt that called `run_gibbs_chain` repeatedly via checkpoint-resume in 20-sweep increments showed an apparent ~1.4 MB/sweep residual — this was a methodology artifact, not a real leak: each resumed call rebuilds and re-traces the `@tf.function`s from scratch, since they're defined as local closures inside `run_gibbs_chain`, so that test was measuring "cost per retrace" not "cost per sweep." ## Real bugs found and fixed (each caught by dense-reference validation, not by trusting derivations)

- **φ-block HMC gradient**: the FD backward pass could re-query a shifted angle into a different interpolation cell, producing wild step-size-dependent gradients — replaced with an analytic bilinear-interpolation gradient; the regression tests' own FD ground truth was also unstable and fixed. Block 3 gradients are production-trustworthy.
- **alm precision convention**: uniform 1/C_ℓ used where the real spherical-harmonic convention requires 2/C_ℓ for m>0 — a genuine variance error, though not the cause of any sampler stall.
- **Messenger conditional derivation**: the plausible-looking naive `t|s,d` precision was subtly wrong (needs the reduced noise N−T); caught against an exact dense reference before touching production.
- **SHT operator normalisation**: AᵀA is not a scalar multiple of identity — needs per-mode m=0/m>0 weights *and* empirical calibration (HEALPix quadrature is ~1–2% off the analytic constant, enough to bias specific modes by tens of posterior SEs).
- **HMC + matrix-free SHT JIT-compile incompatibility**: `jit_compile=True` cannot compile through `tf.py_function`, so the combination had silently never run — a one-line fix unlocked ~0.34s/sweep production HMC (see ROADMAP).
- **`tf.gather`/`IndexedSlices` on a custom-gradient output**: `tf.gather` on a `tf.custom_gradient` op's output produces `IndexedSlices`, not a dense tensor, as the upstream cotangent — any `tf.py_function`/`.numpy()` call inside that op's backward pass must `tf.convert_to_tensor()` it first. Surfaced by the matrix-free masked-likelihood path adding a `tf.gather` on `apply_lensing_tf`'s output (fixed in commit `19a7977`); applies to any future custom-gradient op feeding into `tf.gather`/`tf.boolean_mask`.
- **φ-block memory leak (eager `tf.py_function` registration) — root-caused and fixed, 2026-07-31.** A stale "cannot be traced" assumption from 2026-07-01 (true when written, false after the matrix-free SHT path added proper `tf.py_function` wrapping, never re-checked) left the φ HMC step running eagerly every leapfrog step. Eager `tf.py_function` calls register in TF's process-global eager py-function registry and are never freed, leaking ~362 MB/sweep at `phi_n_lfs=80`. Fixed by tracing `bootstrap_results`+`one_step` together in `@tf.function`, with `cl_phiphi_full` (Block 4's per-sweep-resampled spectrum) promoted from a closed-over numpy array to a `tf.Variable` so the trace doesn't silently freeze it. Full writeup below (Coverage-test prerequisite work). This was the cause of the lmax=300 OOM saga (jobs 11635790/11661265/11663099).
- **Cosmic variance mistaken for sampler bias (caught before it reached production)**: an early draft of the simulation-validation script (`scripts/validate_sim_lmax300_lensing.py`) compared posterior C_ℓ^TT draws against the CAMB *ensemble-average* input spectrum. At low l (few (2l+1) modes per bin) a single realization's power can differ from the ensemble mean by O(1), producing spurious multi-sigma "bias" that was really just cosmic variance. Fixed by comparing against the *realized* power in the actual drawn alm_true (`model.compute_sl_np`, the same S_l the exact C_ℓ|alm conditional targets) — caught via cheap small-scale local testing (lmax=30/nside=32) before the fix was trusted at lmax=300.
- **`integrated_autocorr_time`'s zero-variance short-circuit was a hidden absolute-tolerance bug, found 2026-07-21.** Both `gate_phi_alm_mixing_check.py` and `gate_patch_mixing_check.py` guarded against a constant (already-converged-looking) input with `np.allclose(x, 0.0)` after mean-subtraction — but phi-power summary statistics at production lmax are naturally O(1e-9) to O(1e-17) in absolute magnitude, well inside `np.allclose`'s default `atol=1e-8`, so *any* real fluctuation in phi power was misread as "no variance" and silently short-circuited to a fake ESS=100%. This produced misleadingly perfect phi-block mixing numbers in **both** the lmax≤50 joint-mixing gate (job 11609120, originally reported as "phi mixes well except l=2") and the lmax=300 patch-scale gate (job 11626450, originally "worst ESS 5.47%"). Recomputing from each run's saved `.npz` samples with a scale-relative check (`np.allclose(x / np.abs(x).max(), 0.0)`) gives real phi ESS of 4.0-37.4% (gate 1) and 4.7-11.7% (patch gate) — comparable to the alm block's ESS in both cases, not near-perfect. Both gates' overall MARGINAL verdicts are unchanged (the alm block was already the binding worst case in both), but the phi-block claims in `ROADMAP.md` were corrected in place; the fix is applied to both scripts so future gate runs report real numbers. Lesson: a "no variance" fallback branch needs a tolerance scaled to the data, not a hardcoded absolute one — the same family of bug as the Sokal-estimator misreading non-stationary drift as ESS=100% in the lmax=300 simulation-validation run, just via tolerance rather than window logic.

## Closed-out sampler routes (do not revisit without new evidence)

- **Phase 0b — diagonally-preconditioned CG on the masked sky: abandoned.** The Jacobi preconditioner degrades ~4 orders of magnitude under a realistic mask (f_sky≈0.74); extrapolated cost 10⁴–10⁵ iterations per Gibbs step. Diagnosed in miniature and quantified.
- **Phase 0c — messenger-field sampler: built, validated at small scale, and closed as non-viable at production scale.** The full arc: correct conditionals validated against dense references; the masked-sky divergence traced to off-diagonal AᵀA coupling; a block-diagonal-by-m correction built that matches the exact dense fix's accuracy (the off-diagonal energy is 99.7% same-m with a parity selection rule); production benchmark at m_group_size=5 = 354.68s/sweep. The production run completed but **failed validation — intrinsic critical slowing down** at n_alm≈90k: ESS 0.6%, C_ℓ biased low, monotonic drift. Systematically ruled out as fixes: more inner iterations (would need ~25–85 *hours* per sweep), τ² annealing (refuted by targeted toy tests — the current τ² is already optimal), mask topology, C_ℓ dynamic range, and the block approximation itself (the exact dense correction mixes equally slowly). The slowdown is dimensionality-intrinsic to plain messenger Gibbs — the known failure mode that motivated messenger-as-CG-preconditioner work in the literature.
